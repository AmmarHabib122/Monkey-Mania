Options-based product refactor

Monkey Mania backend (Django/DRF) with Angular front end. Forge session, goal: make it better.

Outcome

The idea survived and got sharper. A waiter can now pick several sauces (as parts of one dish) and add-ons on a single product, and stock, price and returns follow exactly what was picked. Work is staged to protect every bill and product already in the system.

Locked decisions

  • Hierarchy ProductCategory > ProductFamily > Product (variant), refactoring the existing product table in place, with images on category, family, variant and option.
  • Options are mandatory or addon; priced per branch; allowed options are set per variant.
  • Variant columns mandatory_parts and mandatory_qty_total: parts always sum exactly; a total of 0 skips stock use but keeps the pick and its price.
  • One Recipe table (variant or option to material, global across branches) and a MaterialTransaction history with branch, reason and bill link.
  • Each pick is frozen on the bill in ProductBillProductOption; one option can be returned alone, refunded at its own price, with an optional waste flag.
  • Whole-unit money, rounded down; request_id idempotency on orders, additions and returns.
  • One-call GET menu cached in Redis with versioning; image URLs only.

Rejected, and why

  • Parallel old/new product tables or a mapping table: the user wants one table to query.
  • An OptionGroup table and a rules table: a type column and two product columns do the job.
  • Offline order queue: not wanted; online retry with idempotency instead.
  • Per-variant option prices, a flavour option type, table renaming: dropped for now.
  • Two older branches that used many-to-many sauces: outdated, and they cannot store parts.

Weak points that survived

  • Merging per-branch recipes into a global one: collisions are possible on production, so the migration prints every conflict.
  • Stock data is corrupted today, so an opening stock count is required after migration.
  • Production media serving depends on DEBUG being hardcoded to true in the docker branch.
  • An offer-quantity bug (ProductBillSerializers.py:376) is suspected and still needs a test to confirm.

Parked for phase 2

Discounts and offers: specific or generic bundles (buy 2 waffles, get a mango or any drink free), buy-N-get-one-free, and one manual bill discount; one mechanism per bill. Not part of the current plan.
